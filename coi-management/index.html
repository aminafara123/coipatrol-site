COI management: collect, verify, track, renew and audit

Updated November 2026. Written for US construction and property firms.

A folder of PDFs is only the start of COI management. It's a small loop that runs all year: ask for the certificate, check it, track it, chase the renewal and keep proof for the audit. The steps below are written for a small office.

What is COI management?

COI management is the process of collecting, checking and maintaining certificates of insurance from the subcontractors and vendors you hire. It covers five steps: collect, verify, track, renew and audit. The goal is that every company working for you has current insurance that meets your contract, and that you can prove it whenever someone asks.

How do you collect certificates of insurance?

Send a written request before the work starts. Give your exact legal company name, the coverage types and limits you require, and any endorsements such as additional insured. Ask the sub to forward it to their insurance agent, who sends the certificate directly to you. Don't give site access until it arrives.

Our COI request email template has the wording ready to paste.

How do you verify a certificate of insurance?

Compare the certificate to your contract line by line. Check that the insured name matches the company you hired. Check that no policy has expired and that each required coverage is listed at or above your limits. Confirm your company is the certificate holder, and collect the endorsement pages your contract requires.

  • It came from the agent or broker, not typed up by the sub.
  • The dates cover the work, or you have a renewal reminder set.
  • Additional insured status is backed by an endorsement, not only a line in the description box. See additional insured vs certificate holder.

For a field-by-field walkthrough and a free checker, read how to verify a certificate of insurance.

How do you track certificates once they are on file?

Log every policy on every certificate with its expiration date in one list. Give each one a status: green for current, yellow for expiring within 30 days, red for expired or missing. Review the list every week. One owner should be responsible for the list, with a backup when that person is away.

A spreadsheet does this well for a small list. Our free COI tracking spreadsheet has the color rules built in.

How do you handle COI renewals?

Request the renewed certificate about 30 days before each policy expires. Follow up at 14 days and 7 days if nothing arrives, and send a final notice on the expiration date. Copy the agent when you can, because the agent issues the certificate. Never extend an expired certificate on a promise that the policy renewed.

How does COI management help at the insurance audit?

At your annual premium audit, the insurer asks for a current certificate for every sub you paid during the policy year. Payments to subs without one can be added to your own payroll and charged premium. Good COI management means you hand the auditor one complete file instead of digging through an inbox.

The construction insurer BITCO says this on its premium audit page. Without a current certificate, the payroll of the subcontractor's employees is included in your audit. The same page advises keeping current certificates for each subcontractor used during the policy period. To estimate the cost of a missing certificate, use our workers' comp audit calculator.

What tools do you need for COI management?

It depends on how many subs you manage. Under about 10 to 15, a spreadsheet and a calendar can work. Past that, self-serve software that reads certificates and emails renewal reminders saves hours. Firms with hundreds of vendors, or that want certificates verified with the insurer, are better served by an enterprise platform.

What else do people ask about COI management?

People also ask how COI management differs from simple tracking, whether you can hand the whole job to someone else, what a certificate does and doesn't prove, and who is allowed to issue one. For your exact requirements, check your contracts and ask your broker.

Is COI management the same as COI tracking?

Not quite. COI tracking usually means watching expiration dates. COI management is the whole job around it: requesting certificates, checking them against your contracts, chasing renewals, storing endorsements and producing records for audits, owners and lenders. Tracking is the part most firms start with, and the part that most often slips.

Can I outsource COI management?

Yes. Some enterprise platforms sell a full-service option where their staff request, review and chase certificates for you. It costs far more than self-serve software and suits firms with hundreds or thousands of vendors. A small firm with one office admin usually does fine keeping it in house with the reminders automated.

Is a COI the same as an insurance policy?

No. The certificate is informational only. It doesn't amend, extend, or alter the coverage of the policies listed, and the ACORD 25 form says so in its own disclaimer. Coverage rights come from the policy and its endorsements. Keep the endorsements with the certificate, because they are where the coverage promises live.

Who issues a certificate of insurance?

The subcontractor's insurance agent, broker, or carrier issues it, not the subcontractor. A certificate that was typed up or edited by the sub themselves is a fraud red flag: always accept COIs only from the producer listed on the form.
