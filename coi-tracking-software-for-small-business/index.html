COI tracking software for small business: what you actually need

Updated November 2026. Written for US construction and property firms.

Most COI tracking software is built for companies with a risk department and thousands of vendors. A small business with 5 to 50 subcontractors or vendors needs far less. This guide covers what matters, what to ignore and when you do not need software at all. We make CoiPatrol, so weigh that, and check our comparison page too.

What is COI tracking software for small business?

It is software that stores the certificates of insurance you collect from subcontractors and vendors, tracks when each policy expires and reminds them to send a renewal. Small-business tools are self-serve. You sign up without a sales call, see the price up front and set it up yourself, usually in under an hour.

Does a small business need COI tracking software at all?

Not always. With fewer than about 10 to 15 active vendors, a spreadsheet with color rules works if one person checks it every week. Software starts paying off when you chase more renewals than you can remember, when the person who keeps the list is often busy, or after a lapse has already slipped through.

What features does a small business actually need?

Five things matter. The tool should read dates and limits from the ACORD 25 so you do not retype them. It should show what is current, expiring and expired at a glance. It should email renewal requests to your vendors, not only to you. It should log every email, and export everything for an audit.

  1. Certificate reading. Upload the PDF, check the dates it found, done.
  2. A simple status view. Green, yellow and red is enough.
  3. Reminders to the vendor. Sent automatically, under your company name.
  4. A record of every email. Proof that you asked, and when.
  5. An export. One file for your auditor, owner or lender.

Which features can a small business ignore?

Most small firms can skip carrier-direct policy verification, managed review teams, complex approval workflows and integrations with large accounting systems. These are real features that large companies need. For a firm with one office admin and a few dozen vendors, they add cost, setup time and screens you will never open.

Be honest with yourself here. If you have hundreds of vendors, need every certificate verified with the insurer, or want someone else to review them for you, an enterprise platform is the right buy, and our honest comparison of COI tracking software names them.

How should a small business judge COI software pricing?

Look for a published price first. Many enterprise tools only quote after a demo, which usually means a price built for larger firms. Then check whether you pay per vendor or a flat fee, what the free tier covers and what you would pay at twice your size. Ask about setup fees before you sign anything.

How do you compare COI tracking tools quickly?

Use a short test. Sign up for a free plan or trial, upload three real certificates, and see how many dates you had to fix. Then set up one reminder and look at the email your vendor would receive. If either step needs a sales call or a support ticket, that tool is probably built for someone bigger.

  • Is the price on the website?
  • Could you start today without talking to anyone?
  • Do reminder emails go out under your company name?
  • Can you export everything and leave if it does not work out?

How long does it take a small business to get started?

With a self-serve tool, setup takes minutes and loading your active vendors takes an afternoon. Gather the newest certificate for each vendor working now or in the next 90 days. Upload them, confirm the dates and add each vendor's email. Ask for fresh certificates where yours are missing or expired, then turn on reminders.

What else do small businesses ask about COI tracking software?

The questions below cover free plans, per-vendor versus flat pricing, whether software can tell you a policy was cancelled mid-term, and whether a spreadsheet is still good enough. The honest answer to most of them depends on your size. Work it out for today and for twice your current number of vendors.

Is free COI tracking software good enough?

For a few subs, often yes. Free tiers usually cap the number of vendors, and some cap features too. Check that the free plan sends reminders to your subs, not only to you. If you might outgrow it, check what the next plan costs before you load every certificate into it.

Should COI software charge per vendor or a flat price?

Either can work. Per-vendor pricing looks cheap at first and grows with every sub you add. A flat price is easier to budget and does not punish you for keeping old subs on file. Work out the cost at your size today and at twice that size before you decide.

Does COI tracking software check that a policy is still active?

Most small-business tools do not. They read the certificate and track its dates, so a policy cancelled mid-term can still look valid. Some enterprise platforms, and tools that share live policy data from the insurer, can catch cancellations. If you need that, expect a higher price and a longer setup.

Is Excel good enough for COI tracking?

For a handful of subs, yes: a sheet with expiry dates and conditional formatting does the job. It breaks down past a few dozen vendors because it cannot send renewal reminders, chase subs automatically, or flag coverage gaps, and missed renewals happen silently.
