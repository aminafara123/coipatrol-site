Insurance certificate expiration reminders: when to send them and what to say

Updated November 2026. Written for US construction and property firms.

Every certificate of insurance has an expiration date. If nobody asks for the renewal in time, the date passes quietly and you find out at the worst moment. This page gives you a reminder schedule, who to copy, wording you can paste, and an honest view of calendar reminders versus software.

What is an insurance certificate expiration reminder?

It is a message sent before a policy on a certificate of insurance expires. It asks the subcontractor or vendor to have their agent send an updated certificate showing the renewed policy. A good reminder goes out early enough for the agent to act, names the policy and date, and says where to send the certificate.

When should insurance certificate expiration reminders go out?

A common schedule is 30 days, 14 days and 7 days before the expiration date, then one final notice on the day it expires. Thirty days gives the agent time to issue the renewal. The later reminders catch the subs who ignored the first one. Start at 45 or 60 days for subs who are always slow.

  1. 30 days before. A friendly request for the renewed certificate.
  2. 14 days before. A second request. Copy the office admin who owns the list.
  3. 7 days before. A firmer note that names the date and what happens after it. Copy the project or property manager.
  4. On the expiration date. A final notice that the certificate on file has expired and site access is on hold until a new one arrives.

Who should get each expiration reminder?

Send every reminder to the subcontractor's main contact and, if you have it, their insurance agent. The agent issues the certificate, so including them saves a step. Copy your own office admin on the last two reminders. Copy the project or property manager on the final ones, because they control site access.

The agent's name and email are in the producer box at the top of the old certificate. Our guide to what a certificate of insurance is shows where each box sits.

What should an expiration reminder email say?

Keep it short. Name the policy type and the exact expiration date. Ask for an updated certificate from the agent, sent to a specific email address. Say that the certificate holder and additional insured wording stay the same. On the later reminders, state plainly what happens if nothing arrives by the date.

30-day and 14-day reminder

Subject: Updated certificate of insurance needed: [policy type] expires [date]

Hi [Name],

Our records show your [policy type] policy expires on [expiration date]. Please ask your insurance agent to send an updated certificate showing the renewed policy to [your email].

The certificate holder and additional insured wording stay the same as on your current certificate.

Thank you,
[Your name]
[Company] | [Phone]

7-day and day-of notice

Subject: Final notice: [policy type] certificate expires [date]

Hi [Name],

We still do not have an updated certificate for your [policy type] policy, which expires on [expiration date]. Without a current certificate on file we cannot give your crew site access after that date.

Please have your agent send the renewed certificate to [your email] today.

Thank you,
[Your name]
[Company] | [Phone]

What should happen on the expiration date if nothing has arrived?

Mark the policy as expired and treat the sub as uninsured until a new certificate arrives. In practice that usually means no site access and no new work. Tell the project or property manager the same day. Call the agent listed on the old certificate, because the policy may have renewed and nobody sent the paperwork.

Do not let an expired certificate sit for weeks. If the sub has a claim in that gap, it can land on your own policy.

Are calendar reminders good enough for certificate expirations?

For a handful of subs, yes. An Outlook or Google Calendar event 30 days before each expiration works. Calendar reminders only remind you, though. You still write and send every email, log who replied and move each event when a policy renews. Past about 15 subs, that manual work is where renewals get missed.

  • A calendar does not email the sub. You do.
  • It does not know when the new certificate has arrived, so it keeps nagging or you delete the event too early.
  • When the person who owns the calendar is on leave, the reminders go nowhere.

When does reminder software beat a calendar?

Software wins when the reminders need to reach the sub, not just you. It sends each email on schedule, under your company name, stops once a new certificate is uploaded, and keeps a log of every message. That log matters when an owner, lender or auditor asks what you did to collect the certificate.

What else do people ask about certificate expiration reminders?

The questions below cover how far ahead to start, whether to send reminders to the sub or their agent, what to do when a sub says the policy renewed but sends nothing, and how long a certificate stays valid. The short version is simple. Start early, include the agent and accept only a certificate as proof.

How far ahead of expiration should I request a renewal COI?

30 days before the policy expiration date is standard. Some firms start at 45 or 60 days for slow-responding subs. Automated reminders at 30, 14, and 7 days are a common cadence. A day-of notice closes the loop, so nobody can say they were not told.

Should reminders go to the subcontractor or to their insurance agent?

Send them to both if you can. The subcontractor has to approve and pay for the renewal. The agent is the one who issues the new certificate. Sending to both cuts out a round of forwarding. The producer box on the old certificate shows the agent's contact name and email.

What if a subcontractor says the policy renewed but sends nothing?

Thank them and ask for the certificate anyway. A verbal yes is not proof of coverage. Ask the agent listed as producer on the old certificate to send the renewal directly. Until it arrives, record the policy as expired and treat the sub as uninsured for site access.

How long is a COI valid?

A COI has no validity period of its own. It is a snapshot that is only as current as the policy expiration dates printed on it. Most policies run 12 months, so most companies require a fresh certificate at every policy renewal and before each new project.
