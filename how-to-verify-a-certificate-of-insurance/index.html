How to verify a certificate of insurance, field by field

Updated November 2026. Written for US construction and property firms.

A certificate of insurance is easy to receive and easy to file without reading. Check each part of the ACORD 25 form. If anything looks like a fake or edited certificate, confirm the policy with the agent. The free checker below compares a certificate to your requirements and drafts the email asking for a fix.

How do I verify a certificate of insurance?

First, make sure the certificate came from the agent or broker named as producer, not from the sub. Second, compare every coverage, limit and date to the requirements in your contract. Third, if anything looks wrong or the job is large, call the producer and confirm the policy is in force.

    What should I check on each field of the ACORD 25?

    Work from top to bottom. Check that the producer can be contacted, the insured name matches your contract and each insurer is a real insurance company. Then check every coverage, limit and date against your requirements. Finish with the certificate holder box, the endorsement columns and the signature. The table below lists each field.

    Field on the ACORD 25What to checkRed flag
    Date (top right)When the certificate was issuedAn old date on a certificate you asked for this week. The sub may have re-sent an old file
    ProducerThe agent or broker's name, phone and emailNo contact details, so you cannot call to confirm
    InsuredThe legal business name and address on your subcontractA different company, a trade name or a parent company
    Insurers A to FA named insurance company for each letter, with its NAIC numberAn agency name where the insurer should be, or no NAIC number
    Type of insuranceEvery coverage your contract requires is listedA required coverage is missing
    ADDL INSD and SUBR WVDA "Y" where your contract requires additional insured or a waiver of subrogationA "Y" with no endorsement page behind it

    What are the signs of a fake or altered certificate?

    The biggest sign is where it came from. A certificate sent by the sub, not by their agent, deserves a closer look. Other signs are mismatched fonts or spacing, dates that don't make sense, missing policy numbers and a producer you can't reach. Any one of these is a reason to call the agent before you file it.

    1. It came from the sub, not the agent. Ask the agent to send it to you directly.
    2. The text doesn't line up. Different fonts, sizes or spacing in the dates or limits often mean someone typed over a real certificate.
    3. The dates don't make sense. A policy term much shorter or longer than usual with no explanation, or an effective date after your job started.
    4. Policy numbers are missing or repeated. Separate policies from separate insurers should have different numbers.
    5. The producer can't be reached. Look up the agency yourself and call the number you find, not only the one printed on the certificate.
    6. The sub won't give you the agent's name. Without the agent's name you can't confirm the policy.

    Can a certificate of insurance be edited or backdated?

    Anyone with a PDF editor can change a certificate, which is why you should accept one only from the agent or broker named as producer. Backdating a certificate doesn't create coverage, because the certificate itself confers no rights. Only the policy does. If anything looks off, call the producer.

    Every ACORD 25 says this in the box at the top of the form. The certificate "is issued as a matter of information only and confers no rights upon the certificate holder." Coverage comes from the policy and its endorsements, not from the paper in your file.

    How do I confirm a subcontractor's policy is active?

    Call or email the agent or broker named in the producer box. Give the insured's name and the policy number and ask whether the policy is in force today and for the dates of your job. For workers' comp, some state funds and boards also offer an online coverage lookup.

    Here is a short script for the call.

    Hi, this is [your name] from [your company]. We received a certificate of insurance for [insured name], issued by your office on [date]. Can you confirm that policy [number] is in force today, with the limits and dates shown? Is [your company] named as additional insured by endorsement? Could you email me the endorsement page?

    In New York, policyholders of the state fund NYSIF can log in to their account and use "Monitor Subcontractor Coverage" from the Policy menu. NYSIF says that if you enter the sub's FEIN, it "will display if they have current workers' compensation insurance". See NYSIF's subcontractor coverage page. Ask your own carrier whether it offers something similar.

    Does a COI prove I am named as additional insured?

    No. The description box may say you are, but only the endorsement (for example a CG 20 10) or the policy itself proves it. Collect the endorsement along with the certificate. If the sub cannot produce the endorsement, assume you are not covered until they do.

    What else do people ask about verifying certificates?

    People also ask who issues a certificate, what to do with an expired one, whether a certificate is the same as the policy, and whether software can tell you a policy is still active. Use the answers as a starting point, then check your contract and your broker for the exact requirements on your jobs.

    Who issues a certificate of insurance?

    The subcontractor's insurance agent, broker, or carrier issues it, not the subcontractor. A certificate that was typed up or edited by the sub themselves is a fraud red flag: always accept COIs only from the producer listed on the form.

    Can I accept an expired COI?

    No. An expired certificate proves nothing about current coverage. Require an updated certificate showing the renewed policy term before the sub continues work.

    Is a COI the same as an insurance policy?

    No. The certificate is informational only. It doesn't amend, extend, or alter the coverage of the policies listed, and the ACORD 25 form says so in its own disclaimer. Coverage rights come from the policy and its endorsements.

    Does COI tracking software check that a policy is still active?

    Most small-business tools don't. They read the certificate and track its dates, so a policy cancelled mid-term can still look valid. Some enterprise platforms, and tools that share live policy data from the insurer, can catch cancellations. If you need that, expect a higher price and a longer setup.
