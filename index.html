How CoiPatrol works

  1. Add your subs. Type them in one at a time or import your list as a CSV. Any sub with no certificate on file gets a request email automatically.
  2. Subs send their certificates. Each sub gets a private upload link, no account needed. The sub or their insurance agent uploads the ACORD 25 there. Or forward the agent's email to your company's certificate address, and CoiPatrol files it under the matching sub. Anything it cannot match waits in an Unassigned queue for you.
  3. CoiPatrol reads and checks it. It reads the carrier, policy types, policy numbers, limits, dates and the additional insured and waiver of subrogation boxes. Then it checks the certificate against the requirements you set, by trade if you want, and explains any gap in plain words. A clean read is filed at once and you get an email saying what it found. If a date or a limit cannot be read, the certificate waits for you to type it in. If it falls short, the sub is emailed what to fix, with their upload link.
  4. Renewals are chased for you. The sub gets a reminder 30, 14 and 7 days before a policy expires, on the day itself, and once more after a lapse. You are copied from 7 days out. Your wording, your schedule, sent as your company.

Already have a PDF on your desk? Upload it yourself. It goes through the same reading and checks. You get an email whenever a sub uploads, saying whether the certificate meets your requirements, plus a weekly summary of anything that needs attention.

What CoiPatrol reads on a certificate

Most US insurance agents issue the ACORD 25, a one-page certificate of liability insurance. Here are the parts CoiPatrol reads, and what it does with each one. The companies are made up.

  1. 1
    Insured. The sub's company name. When a certificate arrives by email, CoiPatrol uses it to file the certificate under the right sub.
  2. 2
    Insurer. The carrier behind each policy, saved with the policy.
  3. 3
    Type of insurance and policy number. Checked against the policy types you require, for example general liability and workers' comp. A missing type is flagged.
  4. 4
    Policy dates. The expiration date sets the dashboard color, green, yellow within 30 days or red when expired, and starts the reminder schedule.
  5. 5
    Limits. Compared with your minimums. Here it would say: "General liability aggregate is $1,000,000. You require $2,000,000."
  6. 6
    Additional insured and waiver of subrogation. Read as yes or no. If you require them, a certificate without them is flagged and the sub is told.

A lapsed certificate becomes your problem

A certificate of insurance (COI) is the one-page proof that a subcontractor has insurance. You collect one before a sub starts work. Then the policy renews, or gets cancelled, and the paper in your file goes stale.

A sub can hand you a valid COI on Monday and cancel the policy on Friday. If that sub causes damage or someone gets hurt, the claim can land on your own policy. You end up on the hook and carrying the liability.

Missing certificates cost money at audit. Once a year your insurer runs a premium audit. That is the check where they recount your payroll and what you paid subs. If a sub had no workers' comp and you have no current certificate on file, carriers add what you paid that sub to your own premium. The construction insurer BITCO says so on its premium audit page. Common questions

What is a certificate of insurance (COI)?

A certificate of insurance is a one-page document, usually on the ACORD 25 form, issued by an insurance agent or broker. It summarizes a policyholder's coverage: insurer names, policy numbers, coverage types, limits, and effective and expiration dates. It is proof that insurance exists, not the insurance itself.

How do I track subcontractor certificates of insurance?

Collect a COI before the sub gets site access, log the coverage types, limits, and expiration dates, verify them against your contract requirements, and request renewals about 30 days before expiry. A spreadsheet works up to roughly 10 to 15 active subs. Past that, dedicated tracking software prevents missed renewals.

What happens if a subcontractor's insurance lapses during my project?

The risk transfers to you. Injury or damage claims can land on your own general liability policy, lapsed subs can fail owner and lender compliance requirements, and construction lenders often withhold draws until valid certificates are back on file.

How far ahead of expiration should I request a renewal COI?

30 days before the policy expiration date is standard. Some firms start at 45 or 60 days for slow-responding subs. Automated reminders at 30, 14, and 7 days are a common cadence.
