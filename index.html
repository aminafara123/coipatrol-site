A lapsed certificate becomes your problem

A certificate of insurance (COI) is the one-page proof that a subcontractor has insurance. You collect one before a sub starts work. Then the policy renews, or gets cancelled, and the paper in your file goes stale.

A sub can hand you a valid COI on Monday and cancel the policy on Friday. If that sub causes damage or someone gets hurt, the claim can land on your own policy. You end up on the hook and carrying the liability.

Most small firms track this in a spreadsheet and a lot of follow-up emails. Construction managers describe it plainly. Yes, this is how most companies do it. And yes, it is a nightmare.

Missing certificates cost money at audit. Once a year your insurer runs a premium audit. That is the check where they recount your payroll and what you paid subs. If a sub had no workers' comp and you have no current certificate on file, carriers add what you paid that sub to your own premium. The construction insurer BITCO says so on its premium audit page. CoiPatrol's audit export puts every certificate in one file for exactly that moment.

Common questions

What is a certificate of insurance (COI)?

A certificate of insurance is a one-page document, usually on the ACORD 25 form, issued by an insurance agent or broker. It summarizes a policyholder's coverage: insurer names, policy numbers, coverage types, limits, and effective and expiration dates. It is proof that insurance exists, not the insurance itself.

How do I track subcontractor certificates of insurance?

Collect a COI before the sub gets site access, log the coverage types, limits, and expiration dates, verify them against your contract requirements, and request renewals about 30 days before expiry. A spreadsheet works up to roughly 10 to 15 active subs. Past that, dedicated tracking software prevents missed renewals.

What happens if a subcontractor's insurance lapses during my project?

The risk transfers to you. Injury or damage claims can land on your own general liability policy, lapsed subs can fail owner and lender compliance requirements, and construction lenders often withhold draws until valid certificates are back on file.

How far ahead of expiration should I request a renewal COI?

30 days before the policy expiration date is standard. Some firms start at 45 or 60 days for slow-responding subs. Automated reminders at 30, 14, and 7 days are a common cadence.

Enterprise platforms like myCOI and TrustLayer are quote-only and commonly cost several hundred dollars a month. Small-business tools run roughly $29 to $99 a month. CoiPatrol is free for up to 10 subcontractors and $49 a month for up to 50.
