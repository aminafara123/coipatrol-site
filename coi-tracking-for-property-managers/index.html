COI tracking for property managers: vendors, owners and lenders

Updated November 2026. Written for US construction and property firms.

A property manager can have more vendors than a general contractor has subs. Landscapers, cleaners, plumbers, roofers, snow crews, a tenant's movers. Each one needs a current certificate of insurance on file. The list stays manageable when you keep it as one list of vendors across every building you manage.

What is COI tracking for property managers?

COI tracking for property managers means keeping a current certificate of insurance on file for every vendor that works at the properties you manage. You log each policy and its expiration date, check that the right owner and management company are named, and request a renewal before anything lapses. Then you can prove it on request.

The work is the same as for a contractor. The difference is the shape of the list. You deal with many small vendors, many separate owners, and certificates that have to name the right company for each building.

Which vendors should a property manager collect certificates from?

Collect a certificate from every vendor that sets foot on a property, not only the big trades. That includes plumbers, electricians, HVAC techs, roofers, painters, landscapers, snow removal, cleaners, pest control, elevator and fire alarm contractors. It also includes a tenant's movers and contractors when your lease or building rules require it.

Vendors that feel low risk are often the ones missing from the file. A cleaner on a ladder or a landscaper's mower throwing a stone through a window can turn into a claim against the owner.

What insurance do property managers usually require from vendors?

Most property managers ask for general liability, commonly $1 million per occurrence and $2 million aggregate. They add workers' comp for any vendor with employees and commercial auto if the vendor drives for the job. Higher-risk work like roofing may need an umbrella policy. Your management agreement and the owner set the real minimums.

Our subcontractor insurance requirements by trade table lists typical limits for each kind of work. The same table works for vendors.

Who should be named on a vendor's certificate?

Usually both the owner and the management company. The owner is often a separate company for each property, so the vendor's certificate has to name the right one. Ask for them as additional insured, through an endorsement, and as certificate holder. Being listed as certificate holder alone gives no coverage under the vendor's policy.

This is a common mistake on property certificates. Our guide to additional insured vs certificate holder explains the difference in plain words.

How do I track vendor certificates across many properties?

Keep one list of vendors, not one list per building. For each vendor, record every policy, its expiration date and which properties they serve. A vendor that works at six buildings may need a certificate naming six owners. When one policy renews, you update it once and every building is covered again.

  1. Start with vendors working now or booked in the next 90 days.
  2. For each one, note the properties and the owner company of each property.
  3. Check each certificate names the right owner and management company.
  4. Set a reminder 30 days before each policy expires.

What do owners and lenders expect a property manager to show?

Owners expect you to keep uninsured vendors off their property, because a claim from a vendor's accident can land on the owner's policy. Many management agreements put that duty on the manager in writing. When an owner, lender or auditor asks, you should be able to produce a current certificate for any vendor within minutes.

Read your management agreements for the exact wording. Some ask for proof of vendor insurance at set times, such as an annual review. A single export of every vendor, policy and expiration date answers most of these requests.

Should a property management company publish its vendor insurance rules?

Yes, it saves a lot of back and forth. Many property management companies post a vendor requirements page or PDF. It lists the required coverage and limits, the exact names to put on the certificate, the endorsements needed and where to send it. Vendors forward it to their agent, and the certificate arrives right the first time.

What else do property managers ask about vendor certificates?

Property managers most often ask which vendors need a certificate, whether a tenant's movers count, whether a certificate protects the owner, and what to do with an expired one. If your situation is unusual, check your management agreement and ask your broker, because contracts and leases set the real rules.

Do property managers need certificates from every vendor?

Collect one from every vendor that works on site, from landscapers and cleaners to plumbers, roofers and elevator contractors. The vendor that seems low risk is often the one that slips through. A fall from a ladder or a flooded unit can lead to a claim against the owner and the manager.

Should a tenant's movers or contractors give the building a COI?

Many buildings require it, and the lease or the building rules usually say so. Ask for the certificate before move-in day or before work starts, with the owner and management company named the way your rules require. Track it like any other vendor, even if the job only lasts one day.

Does a vendor's COI protect the property owner?

Only if the owner is named as additional insured on the vendor's policy through an endorsement. Being the certificate holder gives the owner no coverage. Ask for the endorsement and name the exact owning entity, which is often a separate company for each property. The certificate alone doesn't prove it.

Can I accept an expired COI?

No. An expired certificate proves nothing about current coverage. Require an updated certificate showing the renewed policy term before the sub continues work. For a vendor, that means no new work orders until the updated certificate arrives from their agent. Mark the vendor as inactive in your list so nobody books them by mistake.
