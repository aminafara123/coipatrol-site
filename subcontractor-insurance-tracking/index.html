Subcontractor insurance tracking for general contractors

Updated November 2026. Written for US construction and property firms.

You collected a certificate before the sub started. Six months later the policy renewed, or lapsed, and the paper in your file says nothing about which. Subcontractor insurance tracking closes that gap. This guide covers what to record, when to collect and how to stay ready for the audit.

What is subcontractor insurance tracking?

Subcontractor insurance tracking means keeping a current certificate of insurance on file for every sub you hire, and knowing when each policy expires. You record each policy, check it against your subcontract, request renewals before they lapse and keep subs without current coverage off the job. It is how a general contractor avoids carrying a sub's risk.

Why does subcontractor insurance tracking matter at premium audit?

Your insurer audits your policy after the year ends. It asks for current certificates for the subs you paid. If a certificate is missing, the insurer can count what you paid that sub as your own payroll and charge premium on it. A gap in your tracking turns directly into a bigger insurance bill.

This comes from the construction insurer BITCO's premium audit page. For workers' comp, it includes the payroll of the subcontractor's employees in your audit when there is no proof of coverage. For general liability, it can include subs who lack a current certificate or whose limits fall short. Tracking the limits matters, not only the dates. Our workers' comp audit calculator estimates what a missing certificate could add.

What should you track for each subcontractor?

Track the sub's legal name, contact and insurance agent. For each policy, record the type, insurer, policy number, effective and expiration dates and limits. Note whether the additional insured and waiver of subrogation endorsements are on file. Also note which jobs the sub is working on, so you know who is affected by a lapse.

  • Policies. General liability, workers' comp, commercial auto and umbrella, as your subcontract requires.
  • Limits. Compare each one to your minimums. Our subcontractor insurance requirements by trade table lists typical figures.
  • Endorsements. Keep the pages themselves, not only the boxes ticked on the certificate.
  • Status. Current, expiring within 30 days, or expired.

When should you collect a subcontractor's certificate?

Collect it before the sub gets site access, every time. Ask again at the start of each new project if the requirements or the named parties change. Then request a renewed certificate about 30 days before each policy expires. A sub who renews on time but forgets to send the paperwork still counts as missing.

How do you track subcontractor insurance across several jobs?

Track the sub once, not once per job. Keep one record for each subcontractor with all their policies, and link it to every job they work on. When a policy renews, update it in one place. When one lapses, you can see straight away which jobs and which project managers need to know.

If a job's owner or lender needs to be named on the certificate, note that against the job. A sub may need a fresh certificate naming each owner.

What should happen when a subcontractor's insurance lapses mid-project?

Stop the sub's work on site until a current certificate arrives. Tell the project manager the same day. Call the agent listed on the old certificate, because the policy may have renewed without anyone sending paperwork. If it truly lapsed, the risk is on you until it is fixed. Lenders may also hold draws.

Our requirements guide explains what a lapse exposes you to in more detail.

Should you track subcontractor insurance in a spreadsheet or software?

A spreadsheet is fine for a small, steady list of subs if one person checks it every week. It cannot email a sub or notice a date turning yellow. Once you are chasing more than about 15 subs, software that reads the certificate and sends renewal reminders costs less than one missed renewal.

What else do contractors ask about subcontractor insurance tracking?

The questions below cover sub-tier subcontractors, 1099 subs and workers' comp, whether a certificate proves additional insured status, and what a waiver of subrogation is. Rules vary by state and by contract. Use these answers as a starting point, then confirm the details with your broker and your subcontract.

Should I track my subcontractors' own subcontractors?

If your subcontract makes your subs pass the same insurance requirements down to their own subs, then yes, ask for those certificates too. This is often called a flow-down clause. An uninsured crew two tiers down can still cause a claim on your site. At minimum, know which companies are working on your jobs.

Do 1099 subcontractors need their own workers' comp?

It depends on your state and on the work. Your workers' comp auditor will still ask for proof. Without a current certificate showing the sub's own coverage, what you paid them can be counted as your payroll. A sole owner with no employees may be exempt in some states, so ask your broker what your state accepts.

Does a COI prove I am named as additional insured?

No. The description box may say you are, but only the endorsement (for example a CG 20 10) or the policy itself proves it. Best practice is to collect the endorsement along with the certificate. If the sub cannot produce the endorsement, assume you are not covered until they do.

What is a waiver of subrogation?

An endorsement in which the subcontractor's insurer gives up its right to recover claim payments from the general contractor or owner after paying a loss. GCs commonly require it to stop a sub's insurer from suing them back. Ask for the endorsement page, because a "Y" in the certificate's SUBR WVD column is only a summary.
